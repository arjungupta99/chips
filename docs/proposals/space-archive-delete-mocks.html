Archive and delete, by swiping a row

Swipe a space's row left and two buttons come out from under it, the way Mail and Messages do it on iOS: Archive and Delete. Tapping either one opens a sheet with the space on it, so you can see which one you're about to act on. Nothing happens until you confirm.

This is a running mock, not the app. You can drag the rows yourself, with a mouse or a finger. The sheets and the rows are drawn to the phone's own sizes and colours.

What “archive” and “delete” can mean here, and what each costs

The swipe and the sheets are the same work whichever options you pick. That part is the phone's list, one gesture and one sheet. The real choice is what the two words do to a room. Two facts drive it.

  • Chips already has half of archive. A room can be closed. Closing takes it off the list, keeps its history, and it can be reopened. That's the Closed · N row at the bottom of the list now, where Jacobian's archived Spaces go. That row would be renamed Archived · N, so it's one word for both.
  • Jacobian reopens rooms. Every time its registry changes (a new Space is enough), it moves every room it opened back in line with its Space. A room you archived by hand for a Space that is still live would come back within hours.

Archive: pick one

A0Close, under a new namenot recommended

The swipe calls the close that already exists. Rooms of live Jacobian Spaces come back at Jacobian's next registry change, so it would look broken within a day.

Cost: phone only, then a phone rebuild. The smallest option, but it doesn't do the job.

A1Archive holds until you unarchiverecommended

Same swipe. A room you archive stays archived whatever its Jacobian Space is doing. A room you unarchive stays open. Rooms you never touched still follow their Spaces, as they do today. This is the rule Chips already has for names: once a person renames a room, a harness's name for it stops applying.

Cost: the server records whether a person or a harness archived the room (one column, migration 0026). The room list Jacobian reads leaves out rooms a person has moved, so Jacobian needs no change. The server also refuses a harness that tries to reopen one. On the desktop, Close space becomes Archive, and its Closed row becomes Archived. Then restart the 4431 server and run pnpm phone.

A2A1, and it archives the Space in Jacobian too

Archiving a room in Chips also archives its Space in Jacobian, and unarchiving restores it. Chips and Jacobian never disagree about what's put away.

Cost: everything in A1, plus a change to Jacobian's adapter: it notices a room a person archived and archives the Space. That needs your go-ahead to edit, commit and restart Jacobian.

Delete: pick one

D1Gone from every list; the server keeps the recordrecommended

The room disappears from every device and from search, for everyone in it, and can't be brought back from the app. The server's log keeps what was said, the same way it does for a deleted message now. If Jacobian speaks in that Space again, a fresh, empty room opens. Direct rooms with a harness can only be archived.

Cost: a third room state, deleted, next to open and closed. It needs no new event type. The rest: the projection and search leave deleted rooms out; the server only lets the room's owner or a workspace owner or admin delete; and a harness's key for the room is freed, so the next message opens a new room. An ADR. It shares A1's restart and rebuild. On the desktop, Delete space… sits next to Archive and opens a dialog with the same wording.

D2Erased from the server

Everything in D1, and the room's messages, files, search entries, read marks and icon are also removed from the database and the disk. Each device drops its cached copy.

Cost: the log stops being the complete record, so rebuilding from it no longer brings the room back. It needs its own ADR on erasure, a sweep over about ten tables and the attachment store, and a cache purge on every device. It's several times D1. I'd hold it until privacy or a teammate asks for it.

What I'd build: A1 + D1. Neither changes Jacobian. Rolling it out takes one migration, a restart of the 4431 server, and a pnpm phone rebuild. I'd walk it on the iPhone simulator first. Android uses the same swipe library but won't be walked unless you ask.
