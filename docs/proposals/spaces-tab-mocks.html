Proposal · phone first

The Spaces tab, plainly

Three changes: the list loses its box and becomes the list an iPhone draws, the thumbnail grows from 44 points to 56, and a space can wear a mark of its own from a library Chips draws itself. Everything below is live — tap the phones. Drawn in the lifted palette (the palette proposal, Lift + respace), so the faces are the respaced eight and the gold, amber and blue are the new ones.

What was asked, and what it costs

  1. Lose the inner box. Today every row sits inside one bordered, rounded card with the page showing round it. iOS lists do not do that; Messages, Mail and Settings' plain lists run their rows to both edges and separate them with a hairline that starts where the text starts. Cost: small. It is a stylesheet change in one file.
  2. A bigger thumbnail. 44 → 56 points. The row stays 80 tall, so the list holds nine rows on an iPhone 15 instead of ten. Cost: small. One token and the separator inset that follows it.
  3. A mark of your own. A long press on a row, Change icon…, and a sheet of Chips' own marks with a colour under them. Cost: the real one — 84 icons to draw, a picker to build, and a decision about where the choice is kept (§6). The cheap version keeps it on this phone; the honest version puts it on the log so the desktop and everyone else sees it too.

3 · Choosing an icon

A long press on the row is where this lives — it is where iOS puts everything that is about a row rather than in it, and the tab already has nothing else competing for the gesture. The sheet that opens is the whole library, a colour strip, and a live preview of the row you are changing.

4 · The library

Eighty-four marks in eight families, drawn for Chips in the same 24-point box and the same 1.75 stroke as the tab bar's. Tap any one to put it on Santa Barbara 2026 in the phone above.

5 · How they are drawn

The ask was for icons unique to Chips. That means drawn here, to house rules, rather than pulled from SF Symbols or a public set — which also means no licence to carry, no dependency to bump, and no chance of the app looking like everybody else's.

  1. A 24-point box, a 20-point live area. Same as ICON_BOX today, so a mark drops into the tab bar, a menu or a row without being redrawn.
  2. One weight: 1.75, round cap, round join. No fills, ever — a filled mark beside a stroked one is the thing that makes a set look bought rather than drawn.
  3. Built on a 2-point grid, with 45° and 90° preferred and curves struck from whole-point centres. Diagonals that are not 45° are allowed once per mark.
  4. Optical, not geometric, weight. A circle at r=9 and a square at 18 read the same size; the square gets pulled to 17.
  5. No text, no flags, no brands, no faces. A mark that needs a letter in it is a label.
  6. The snack family is the house joke and it earns its place. Chips is a snack and a wafer; crisp, cup, bowl, cookie and donut sit beside wafer, circuit and radar on purpose. Nobody else's icon set has that pair of shelves, and it is the cheapest way for the library to be recognisably ours.

6 · Where the choice lives

This is the only real decision in the proposal, and it is the one that costs server work. The smallest thing first:

OptionWhat it costsWhat you getWhat you do not
A. This phone onlyNothing on the server. A row in the phone's SQLite cache, a preference like appearance.The whole feature, today, on the phone.Your icons are gone on a new phone, absent on the desktop, invisible to everyone else.
B. Yours, everywhereA per-person setting on the server, beside the notification settings from M5.6. One route, no new event family.Your icons follow you to the desktop and to a new phone.A teammate still sees the default; two people can disagree about what a room looks like, which is arguably correct.
C. The space's ownA space.icon_set event, a migration, the wire, the desktop sidebar, and a rule about who may set it.One room, one face, for everybody — the way a title already works.Half a day rather than an hour, and a small argument about whether a guest may repaint the room.

I would build A now and B next, and leave C until somebody asks for it. A is an hour and gets the whole of §3 in front of you on the phone tonight; B is the natural follow-on because the icon is really a preference about how you read your list, and the notification settings already prove the shape. C is the only one that needs an ADR, and nothing in the ask demands it.

7 · What I would build

  1. The list. Drop styles.group, take the rows full width, inset the hairline to 84, put the chevron in, grow the face to 56 and the row to 80. One file: apps/mobile/src/spaces/List.tsx. Its tests assert rows and unread counts, not the box, so they should stand.
  2. The avatar. A new row size at 56 in Avatar.tsx, plus an icon prop that swaps the initials for a mark and takes a ground colour. Shape and ring stay as they are.
  3. The library. 75 new marks into apps/mobile/src/ui/icons.ts as LIBRARY_ICONS, kept apart from SYSTEM_ICONS so the drift test against the web keeps checking only what the web has.
  4. The picker. One sheet component over the existing sheet shape (24pt corner, 0.4 scrim), the colour strip, the grid, the live row preview.
  5. The store. Option A: a small keyed preference beside appearance.ts.
  6. The desktop. Untouched under A. Under B, the sidebar row reads the same preference and the same marks move into @chipschat/ui.

Nothing here touches the room, the composer, the viewer or the wire. If §6 stays at A, this is a phone-only change in four files.
