Glass at both ends of the room

Messages has no shelves. The bar at the top and the field at the bottom are translucent material over the conversation, the conversation runs the whole height of the glass and passes under both, and the only hairline is the one that appears when something is actually behind it. Today Chips draws two opaque shelves with the room boxed between them. Four frames, at phone width, each scrolling so you can watch a bubble go under the chrome.

The frames scroll on their own — drag one to scroll it yourself, and it carries on when you let go.

Everything below is CSS backdrop-filter standing in for iOS material.

What each one actually costs

  • The top is free. The bar is the platform's already, so the whole change is three options on the room's Stack.Screen: headerTransparent, headerBlurEffect: "systemChromeMaterial", headerShadowVisible: false. react-native-screens 4.26 has all three; nothing is added to the app.
  • The bottom needs a dependency. Nothing in the app can blur a React Native view. It wants expo-blur, which is a native module — so a rebuild of the build on your phone (pnpm phone) and of the emulator's before either of them shows it.
  • The room has to give back the inches the chrome covers. The list is inverted, so the space under the composer is paddingTop on its content and the space under the bar is paddingBottom, and both have to be measured from the real heights rather than guessed — get it wrong and the newest line sits behind the glass, which is exactly the bug the glass is meant to avoid.
  • Reduce Transparency has to win. iOS has the setting, and when it is on this must go back to the opaque bar it is today — one AccessibilityInfo read, two renders.
  • Android's blur is experimental. expo-blur needs experimentalBlurMethod there and costs frames on a scrolling list. The honest answer on the emulator is a flat translucent surface, not a blur, and the swipe walk will say which.
  • Contrast wants re-checking once, not arguing about. Message, or /command is ink-4, and over a violet bubble sliding under 72% glass it will be thinner than it is over the flat page.

My call

A. It is the iMessage thing you asked for and it is the smallest edit that gets there: the top is three screen options, the bottom is the composer's existing bar with its ground swapped for a BlurView and the list given an inset. The attachment strip, the refusal notice and the closed-space line all stay exactly where they are, on the same glass — they are already children of that bar.

B is the iOS 26 shape and it is a bigger job than it looks: with no bar behind it, the strip of staged files, the notice and the This space is closed line each need a ground of their own, and the + outside a floating capsule has nothing to sit on. Worth doing later, on purpose, not as part of this.

C is there to show what the free half looks like alone. It is better than today and it reads as half-finished: a room that runs under the top and stops dead at the bottom draws the eye to the one shelf left.
