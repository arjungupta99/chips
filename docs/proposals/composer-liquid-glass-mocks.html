The field in glass, and no bar behind it

Today a frosted strip runs the width of the screen behind the composer, so the foot of the room still reads as a bar. Here that strip is gone and the conversation runs to the bottom edge of the glass. The field floats over it as one piece of Liquid Glass, iOS 26's material, which is what Messages uses on your phone. The room scrolls by itself so you can watch what passes under the field. Tap B's field or its + to see the glass respond to a finger.

A browser can't draw real Liquid Glass. The edge lensing here is an SVG stand-in, which only works in Chrome and the desktop app; Safari shows the blur without the lensing.

What B is made of

  • The glass is the system's, and it's already on your phone. expo-glass-effect's GlassView puts UIKit's own UIGlassEffect behind the field. That is the same material as Messages, with real lensing, a rim that catches the light, and a tint that turns dark over dark content without being told. expo-router already depends on it, so it is compiled into the build on your iPhone (ExpoGlassEffect 57.0.1 in Podfile.lock). The change is JavaScript only, plus a rebuild through pnpm phone build as usual.
  • The glass responds to a finger. With isInteractive on, it swells and brightens under your thumb the way iOS 26's own buttons do. B's field and + do the same in this mock.
  • Where there's no Liquid Glass, the field stays as it is today. Below iOS 26, with Reduce Transparency on, on Android and on the web, you get today's capsule: chrome blur or plain paint. The strip stays gone in every case.
  • What used to stand on the strip needs its own ground. A staged photo's chip, a refused command's sentence and This space is closed all sit above the field. Today the strip is what keeps them readable. Without it, each of them gets the same glass as the field. That's in the cost.
  • The top of the room is left as it is. iOS already draws the back button and the connection mark in its own Liquid Glass. The room's name is still in today's chrome capsule, so after B it would be the one piece of glass in a different material. Moving it over is the same change, and I'd suggest doing it next, but I left it out here because you asked about the bottom.

My call

B. It's what you described, and it costs little because the native glass is already in the build. It's about half a day: change the capsule to use the system glass, move the + out beside the field, remove the strip, and give the chips and the notice their glass. A is the ten-minute version. It removes the strip but keeps today's frosted field, which turns dull over a busy photo. It only makes sense if you want to see the room without the strip before deciding on the glass.
