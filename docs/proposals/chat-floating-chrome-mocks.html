Floating chrome, and the blur that fades

No bars at either end — the back, the name and the field float over the room as their own pieces of glass, and behind them the room blurs at the very edge and comes clear a finger's width in. That fade is iOS 26's own scroll edge effect, and it turns out react-native-screens already hands it to us: scrollEdgeEffects: { top: 'soft', bottom: 'soft' } on the room's screen, applied by UIKit to the list itself. Three arrangements of the floating pieces, all with the same fade behind them.

Toggle Edge to see the three values the API actually takes: soft · hard · hidden.

CSS layered backdrop-filter standing in for UIScrollEdgeEffect.

Where each piece comes from

  • The fade is native, and free. scrollEdgeEffects is a screen option in the stack this app already runs (expo-router 57's fork, react-native-screens 4.26) and it reaches UIKit's UIScrollEdgeEffect. No package, no rebuild — it would reach your phone over Metro. soft is the fade; hard is the flat pane with a cut-off; hidden is nothing.
  • It is iOS 26 and only iOS 26. Below that, and on Android, the option is ignored and the capsules float over an unblurred room — which is legible over a bubble and thin over a photo. The fallback is a scrim behind each capsule, which we would want anyway.
  • The list is inverted, and that is the one thing I cannot promise from here. UIKit is told about a scroll view whose top is our bottom. Whether top lands on the name or on the field is a five-minute answer on the simulator and a swap of two words if it is the wrong way round.
  • The capsules need expo-blur — or they don't. Real glass (translucent, blurring what passes under the capsule itself) is expo-blur and a rebuild. Painted capsules — surface, a hairline, a soft shadow — need nothing, and over the fade they read almost the same. A and C are drawn painted; B is drawn as glass, so you can see the difference between them.
  • A floating field gives up the ground the extras stand on. The staged-file chips, a refused command's sentence and This space is closed are children of the composer's bar today. They need their own capsule above the field — drawn here in C, since that is the arrangement that has room for it.
  • The title is the platform's in A, ours in B and C. Keeping the native header (transparent, no blur of its own) keeps the chevron, the truncation, Dynamic Type and the push animation. Drawing our own capsule means owning all four — and it is the only way the name gets glass around it.

My call

A, then decide about the name. It is a day's work at most and most of it is deleting: the header goes transparent, the composer's bar loses its ground and becomes a capsule, the list gets its insets, and one screen option turns on the fade. On iOS 26 the chevron and the connection mark already arrive in the system's own glass capsules, so the top is floating without this app drawing a single bar.

B is the full version of what you asked for — everything in glass, including the name — and it is the one that needs expo-blur and a rebuild, and it is the one where the room's own text has to survive under a name that is now a solid object in the middle of it. Worth it if the name-in-glass is the thing you want; I would rather see A on the phone first.

C is A with the extras solved: the + inside the field, and one capsule above it that the chips, the notice and the closed line can all live in.
