The send's curve, and a photo's send

Since the settle, everything that lands at the foot of the room moves the room on one spring: your words, the thinking bubble coming up, the reply. You said it's too quick. The three phones below run the same minute — a message, the harness thinking, its reply, then a photo — on three springs. The left one is the spring on your phone now. Turn on slow motion to see the shape of each.

The motion is not a stand-in: these are the springs the app would run. The photo's flight is new in all three — today a photo lands with a fade.

What a spring's numbers mean

  • The response is how long one swing takes. It sets the pace: the room is most of the way in about half of it, and still there at the end of it.
  • The damping is how much it overshoots. At 0.78 it goes a point or two past and comes back; at 0.9 it barely does. Messages sits near 0.8.
  • Everything shares it. The words, the room, the thinking bubble opening, the reply, and now the photo all move on the one spring, so they read as one body. Changing it changes them all.

The photo

  • The staged thumbnail becomes the tile. It leaves the chip at its own forty points, grows to the tile's size as it rises, and its corner rounds from the chip's to the tile's. The room rises under it on the same spring, and the strip closes behind it.
  • The tile is held until the picture is on it, as your words' bubble is, and the time comes up with it.
  • Words and a photo together would fly as one: the words' bubble above, the picture under it, both from where they sat. Not drawn here; it follows from the two.

My call

The middle one, 0.55 s at 0.82. Today's spring was chosen for the words alone, and a copy of a one-line bubble can afford to be brisk. Now it moves the whole room, a reply, a photo, and the same pace reads as a snap. Half a second is where the room is seen to move rather than jump, and it's still done before you'd wait for it. The slow one is lovely on a photo and a little sleepy on a one-word reply.
