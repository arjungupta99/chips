The time that leaves a bubble

When a second message joins the one before it, that one's time goes: the time sits on the last bubble of a run. On your phone it goes in one frame, and the bubble's foot and its rounded corner go with it — about 15 pt, filmed on the simulator. The three phones below run the same minute: two messages from you in a row, then two from the harness. Slow motion shows the shape of each.

Now on your phone

The time and the foot go the moment you tap Send, while the words are still in the field.

A · The foot tucks up recommended

The time fades and the bubble closes up under its words — shorter, and narrower too where the time was the widest line (a "Wait", an "On it.") on the spring of what lands under it: the send's for yours, so it moves with the room and the flight; the arrival's for a harness's first reply when its second comes in. The corner closes as the foot arrives.
Cost: about 100 lines in Line.tsx — the old bubble behind the new one, shrinking to it, and its time in front. Transforms only: nothing is laid out per frame, so nothing new for the frame drops. The bubble is measured twice, as it was and as it is, when its time leaves. (Built as ADR 0073: in the app a narrower bubble of yours also slides home from its old left edge, so its words stay put, which this mock does not show.)

B · Every bubble keeps its time

Nothing leaves, so nothing jumps, as in Telegram. A run of four says the same minute four times. The foot's corner still closes in one frame, 13 pt of rounding.
Cost: one line. Undoes "the time on the last alone" from the bubble design (ADR 0050).
