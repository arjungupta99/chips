# Security policy

Chips brokers decisions about actions that touch the real world — sending mail, moving money,
deleting files. We take reports seriously and we would rather hear from you early.

## Reporting a vulnerability

Report it privately on GitHub, with
[Report a vulnerability](https://github.com/arjungupta99/chips/security/advisories/new) on the
repository's Security tab, where only you and the people who make Chips can read it. Or email
**security@chipschat.ai**. Either way, send:

- what you found and where,
- the steps to reproduce it,
- what an attacker could do with it,
- anything you need from us to keep testing.

Please do not open a public issue, and please do not test against a server you do not own.

If you would like to encrypt a report by email, say so in a first message with no details and we will
send you a key.

## What to expect

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Within 3 working days | We acknowledge the report and give you a tracking reference. |
| Within 10 working days | We confirm the issue, tell you our assessment of severity, and share a rough fix timeline. |
| At the fix | We credit you in the release notes unless you would rather we did not. |

We ask for 90 days before public disclosure, and we will usually be much faster than that.

## Supported versions

Fixes go into `main` and the next release. Only the newest release is supported, so update to it
to get a fix.

## Scope

In scope: the server (`chipsd`), the CLI, the SDKs, the adapters, the relay, the web, desktop
and mobile clients, and the Agent Connect protocol itself — in particular anything that lets a
party read events from a workspace it is not a member of, resolve an approval it may not
resolve, escalate a harness's autonomy or policy, forge an audit record, or read an attachment
without a valid signed URL.

Out of scope: findings that need physical access to an already-unlocked device, denial of
service by brute volume against a self-hosted server, and reports generated by a scanner with
no demonstrated impact.

## What a relay can still do

A server with a relay hands out invites and pairing codes as links to a page the relay serves, and
that page's script reads the code from the link. Since 25 October 2026 such a link only asks: the
device that uses it waits until the person who sent it allows it, comparing six digits made from
the device's key ([ADR 0232](docs/decisions/0232-a-link-asks-before-it-lets-anyone-in-and-lives-30-minutes.md)),
and no link lasts more than 30 minutes. A dishonest relay page can still send a person to another
server dressed as this one: the page hands the app the server's key along with the code, and a page
that swaps both sends the app wherever whoever runs the relay chooses. Only trusting the relay, or
not opening links in its page (pasting the link into Chips, or scanning it there), prevents that.

## Our side of the deal

Chips holds decisions, never credentials. Harness tokens are stored hashed and compared in
constant time; secrets never appear in logs; attachment URLs are signed, short-lived and bound
to the requesting token; and the audit log is append-only. If you find somewhere that is not
true, that is exactly the kind of report we want.
